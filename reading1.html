Reflection #1: "In Plato's Cave" from On Photography by Susan Sontag

To me capturing images means to collect memories, whereas creating graphics means to show my personality and creativity. I think many tend to forget how precious images can be, especially when throwing away pictures that they find to be meaningless. I've thrown away numerous pictures I claim to not need or don't interest me in the slightest, and then eventually come to regret throwing that picture away when the time comes and I become too fond of it. Although it's a terrible habit since it started my obsession to hoard so many items I believe to be valuable to me or can be valuable to me over time, I feel that I could see glimpses of my world through them that I can go back to and cherish. As Sontag puts it nicely, photographs give us “the sense that we can hold the whole world in our heads as an anthology of images” (Sontag, 3).

Second, creating graphics (or just creating in general) shows my sense of personality, or when looking at another's medium shows me their sense of personality. When it comes to taking pictures, I usually have an ideal theme in my head. The theme is never clear, but I usually always ask myself these questions: why this angle? Why these colors? Why these contrasts and lighting? Why is this object or person included (intentionally or unintentionally) in the picture? What makes it so important? Most of these questions usually never get answered, only a couple or two, but I don't look for those answers and just let the photo I've taken answer it for them. For example, when I had taken a picture of my father in our new home after moving to a new neighborhood, I posed the question to myself: What makes this so important to me? And looking back at that picture, I already had my answer. It was the pride I felt for my father finally achieving his goal to buy a new home instead of living in a cramped apartment for eternity, yet at the same time painful for the loss of the place I was familiar with. Which was that cramped apartment and the people I've befriended alongside it.

Through photography and illustrations, you're able to represent the problems and situations of our world through what you want to perceive it as or what you think about it. I have always thought that the human mind works wonders to help us think and reflect on what we can put on a canvas, and the sheer power of creativity is the most powerful factor of being a human overall. Sontag summarizes this quite nicely when she states that the omnipresence of cameras suggests that “time consists of interesting events, events worth photographing” (Sontag, 11). Everyone has something they fancy and find interesting to the point they'd want to save it in either a photograph or draw in a canvas to cherish. Tying back to not only memories, but also the fact that someone could find something so precious about an object or person of interest that would seem so mundane to most people and make something beautiful out of it. Creativity is the power that could help you represent the notions of our world whether through photography, illustration, or many other forms of art; and it is what you think or perceive about this particular moment that can make it valuable and eventually important to not only you but so many others in the future.
