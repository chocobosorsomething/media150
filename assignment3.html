Homework 3 + 4:Photomontage

For this photomontage, I decided to explore the themes regarding the drawbacks of pursuing art. In the continuously changing and advancing world that is modern society (our today), often times we do not have the choice to be able to pursue a passion and make it into a career. That category often tends to fall in the arts. I have witnessed many of my peers who were once passionate about art, especially since many tend to create art either as a hobby or even a passion, never tend to make it out as an artist. In most cases, they would be discouraged from pursuing it and tend to pursue another career that can secure their future, especially when time is always ticking, and to (often times in adulthood) marry and start a family. This piece I created represents that growing expectation: how someone’s passion can die off because of the pressure of needing to eventually marry and ‘grow out’ of your passion for the arts.
Most of the images I used for this photomontage are from the Public Domain Image Archive and Wikimedia Commons. For the background, I played a bit with contrast and brightness. With the flowers on the right, I increased the contrast slightly (alongside the bride) to emphasize this idea of marriage being important and slowly becoming the main focus compared to the left. I increased the brightness on the left to emit a fading sensation of the colorful piece slowly disappearing as the roses and bride become more prominent. Around the edges, and where the woman’s head (acting like an omnipotent figure) is looking at the main subject (who is looking up and holding the paintbrush; being the artist), are the vector shapes that also resemble the growing expectations of falling in line with marriage and taking over the main subject, especially with the eyes looking at the main subject. Lastly, with the clock hovering below the large woman's head, I played a bit with the color balance (alongside masking this particular layer) to change the clock’s color and make it more abnormal, so it would stand out, representing my earlier statements on how the clock is always ticking.
