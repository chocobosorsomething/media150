Reflection #4: Artist Talk with Sara Stern

Sara Stern's works and the passion she put behind them truly blew my mind. One of the projects that she showed that I particularly liked was her thesis project, 'Night Talk.' This particular project stood out to me because of its scale and the context Stern added to this work. Stern gave a brief background to this project, explaining how this building reflected the complex relationship between Columbia University, its board (higher-ups), the staff, and the students. She mentions how the University often installs new buildings with modern architecture but completely fails to meet the needs of the students and the staff after a long while of issues happening within the institution. Whether it be the lack of funding or just the lack of attention brought to the students and the staff working there.

I thought it was rather creative to make it so that this building the institution recently finished creating was sentient. Sentient in a way that it has moving eyes and a mouth that blink and talk, having the ability to voice out its inner thoughts like 'I am a building' or 'Have a good day.' If I were a passerby seeing this building (Lenny) 'look' around and even 'talk' as if we were friends, I would feel a bit spooked out but at the same time rather fascinated. I love the concept of a sentient building able to look around and just talk to people passing by, especially when that building has a particular history to it. I always ask myself these questions whenever I look at buildings that have aged over the years (or even centuries): what if these walls could talk? What have they witnessed or seen over the years that would be quite compelling? If they were able to talk, I feel that we would be able to solve more mysteries that were never solved or even get more context on what daily life was like.

Overall, I feel Night Talk was certainly a compelling work as a standalone, as I've never seen a project on this kind of scale. The idea behind it was impressive and creative, and I certainly found Lenny very endearing. Especially when he blinks, looks around, or makes certain expressions with his mouth (when he gets sad, happy, or sometimes bored). Stern's other works were also very interesting as well, especially Mirror Ball and its stop-motion, but Night Talk definitely stood out to me in terms of the numerous interpretations behind it. I may be wrong in my interpretation, but the background context of Columbia University's failure to sometimes provide for the students and the faculty there, despite the constant new construction of buildings (like where Night Talk took place), made me think that Lenny the building represents the inner thoughts of those faculty and students who felt neglected by how the institution has been run. Whenever I see Lenny get sad so suddenly, it pulls at my heart, like I want to comfort him whenever that happens.
